Read your workout history without losing the context

One number cannot describe a whole training routine. Weight, repetitions, consistency and your notes each tell part of the story. A useful history keeps those details together instead of reducing every session to a personal record.

Compare the same exercise

Start with the same movement and equipment. A machine’s weight label is not automatically comparable to a different machine, and a new variation can change the meaning of a result. Keep those changes in your notes. Compare the number of completed sets and reps alongside the load instead of looking at the weight alone.

Look beyond a single session

Use your workout history and weekly reports to see which sessions you completed and where your routine changed. A missed session or a lower number can have context that a graph does not show. If you are following a coach’s program, bring that context to your review rather than changing the plan based on one data point.

Keep measurements and photos consistent

Vazne brings body weight and measurements alongside training history. Its Mirror feature lets you compare progress photos. For a useful visual record, choose a similar camera position, lighting and pose each time. Measurements and photos are personal records; treat them as context rather than a diagnosis or a promise of a particular result.

Use the record in your next conversation

Review your training cycle, repeated notes and session history before talking with your coach. A specific observation, such as “I completed fewer reps in the last set across three sessions,” is easier to discuss than “progress feels slow.” Vazne’s cycle reports help bring the record together. The AI coach can answer questions using workout context and history, but it does not replace individualized professional advice.
