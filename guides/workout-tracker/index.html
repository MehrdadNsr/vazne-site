How to keep a useful gym workout log

A workout log should help you remember what actually happened in the gym. A planned set and a completed set are different things: your log is the record of the work you did.

What to record for each exercise

Keep the exercise name, weight, completed repetitions and number of sets together. Add a short note when something affects the comparison, such as using a different machine or changing an exercise variation. Choose a consistent way to record weight. For a barbell, that usually means the bar plus all plates; for dumbbells, make clear whether your number refers to one dumbbell or the pair.

Log the set while it is fresh

Record each set before moving on rather than trying to reconstruct the session at home. In Vazne, choose the weight and reps, then log the set. The rest timer and session notes keep the next step close at hand. The interactive example on our home page lets you try the weight selector, repetition controls and set button without creating an account; that website example does not save your data.

A simple example

Suppose you complete three bench-press sets at a total load of 40 kg: 10, 10 and 8 repetitions. Record those three results separately. Writing “40 kg, 3 × 10” would hide the difference between your plan and what you completed. If you changed the bench or stopped early, a brief note makes the entry more useful next time.

Use the last session as context

Before the next workout, review the same exercise and its notes. Compare like with like: the same exercise, equipment and weight convention. Your history is a reference for discussing the next step with your coach, not an instruction to increase weight every session.
